Football Quiz App — Initial Product and Engineering Brief
1. Product Vision
The app is a football quiz experience designed to hook users as quickly as possible.
The first experience must be fast, lightweight, and engaging. A new user should be able to open the app and start answering quiz questions immediately, without needing to create an account, log in, provide an email address, or go through a long onboarding flow.
The goal is simple:
Get the user into the app as fast as possible and make them feel that the app understands their football knowledge, favourite leagues, and level of interest.
2. First-Time User Experience
When the user opens the app for the first time, the app should immediately guide them into a short and engaging quiz flow.
The first session should avoid friction. There should be no mandatory login, no email requirement, and no heavy setup before the user experiences the core value of the app.
A possible first-time flow:
1. User opens the app.
2. User is shown a very short football quiz immediately.
3. The quiz contains around three questions:
   * Question 1: easy
   * Question 2: medium
   * Question 3: advanced
4. Based on the user’s answers, the app estimates their football knowledge level.
5. The app then suggests leagues, teams, players, or topics that may interest them.
6. The app asks lightweight personalization questions only after the user is already engaged.
The app should feel intelligent from the first interaction.
3. Onboarding Goals
The onboarding should be optimized for user engagement and retention.
The app should quickly discover:
* Which football leagues the user follows
* Which national team or country they care about
* How knowledgeable they are
* Whether they are a casual fan or a serious follower
* Which types of quiz questions they enjoy
* Whether they are interested in transfers, player values, goals, gossip, or league tables
The onboarding should not feel like a form. It should feel like part of the game.
4. User Knowledge Levels
The app should classify users into simple football knowledge levels.
Possible levels:
* Newbie — knows famous players and clubs but does not follow football deeply
* Casual Fan — watches major matches and follows some big teams or players
* Intermediate Fan — follows one or more leagues regularly
* Daily Follower — keeps up with results, transfers, tables, and player news
* Advanced Fan — understands multiple leagues, player form, transfers, tactics, and current football context
The app should adapt question difficulty based on the user’s level.
5. Personalization
The app should personalize the experience around the user’s interests.
Important personalization signals:
* User’s country or nationality
* Favourite league
* Favourite club
* Favourite players
* Knowledge level
* Recent quiz performance
* Interest in transfers
* Interest in player market values
* Interest in goals and match results
* Interest in football gossip or Reddit-style discussions
The app should probably show the user content related to their nation early, because this creates immediate emotional connection. However, not every country has a strong national league, so the app needs to handle this intelligently.
For example:
* If the user is from England, Spain, Italy, Germany, France, Brazil, Argentina, Poland, Turkey, or the Netherlands, the national league may be relevant.
* If the user is from a country with a weaker or less-followed league, the app may instead focus on the national team, famous players from that country, or leagues where players from that country currently play.
6. Quiz Content
The quiz should include different types of football questions.
Possible categories:
* Identify the player from a photo
* Recent match results
* Top scorers from the last week
* Current league table positions
* Transfer rumours
* Confirmed transfers
* Player market values
* Club history
* National team questions
* Champions League questions
* Current form questions
* “Who scored?” questions
* “Which club does this player play for?” questions
* “Which player has the higher market value?” questions
* Reddit-style football gossip questions
The quiz should mix easy, medium, and advanced content.
The quality of the quiz questions is one of the most important parts of the app.
7. Data Sources
The app may use multiple data sources to generate or support quiz content.
Possible data categories:
* Players
* Clubs
* Leagues
* National teams
* Fixtures
* Results
* Goals
* League tables
* Transfers
* Player market values
* Recent news
* Reddit discussions
* Football gossip
* Social media trends
Transfer data and recent football events are especially important because they make the app feel current.
8. Core Product Principle
The app should feel alive and up to date.
A football quiz app is much more engaging if it asks about things that happened recently, not only timeless trivia.
The strongest hook may be:
“Do you really know what happened in football this week?”
This could make the app feel more like a daily habit than a one-time trivia game.
9. Engineering Background
The founder has 25 years of software development experience and strong experience building large-scale backend systems.
Technical background:
* Senior software engineer experience in major global software companies
* Deep backend engineering experience
* Experience scaling systems to around 1 billion requests per day
* Strongest languages: Java and Kotlin
* Strong JVM engineering background
* Limited production experience with mobile apps
This affects the technical strategy. The project should lean into strong engineering practices, high-quality architecture, and robust tooling from the beginning.
10. Platform Strategy
The app should be multiplatform.
The same product should work across:
* iOS
* Android
* Potentially other platforms in the future
A cross-platform approach is preferred, but quality must not be sacrificed.
The chosen technology should support:
* Excellent user experience
* Strong testability
* High code quality
* Good performance
* Good battery efficiency
* Maintainable architecture
* AI-assisted development with strong guardrails
11. Code Quality Requirements
Code quality is the number one engineering priority.
Some product trade-offs are acceptable, but poor code quality is not acceptable.
The codebase should be:
* Well tested
* Well structured
* Maintainable
* Easy to reason about
* Safe for AI-assisted development
* Protected by strong local quality checks
* Built in a framework or architecture that reduces the chance of mistakes
The development process should assume that a large amount of code may be written with AI assistance. Therefore, the codebase should have strong guardrails that make it difficult for AI-generated code to introduce bugs, poor patterns, or architectural drift.
12. Local Development Pipeline
The local development pipeline should enforce very high quality before code is pushed.
GitHub should mainly be used for deployment and repository hosting, not as the first line of quality control.
Quality checks should happen locally before pushing.
The local pipeline should include checks such as:
* Unit tests
* Integration tests where appropriate
* Code coverage checks
* Static analysis
* Linting
* Formatting
* Dependency vulnerability scanning
* Type checking
* Architecture rules
* Dead code detection
* Complexity checks
* Security checks
* Build verification
The goal is that low-quality code should not even leave the developer’s machine.
13. Performance and Battery Requirements
The app must use device hardware responsibly.
It should never feel heavy, wasteful, or annoying to the user.
The app must not:
* Drain battery unnecessarily
* Overuse network requests
* Overuse background processing
* Feel slow or bloated
* Make the phone hot
* Become a liability that users want to uninstall
Performance and battery efficiency are product features, not just technical concerns.
14. UX Priorities
Great UX is one of the most important parts of the app.
The app must feel:
* Fast
* Fun
* Intelligent
* Lightweight
* Personal
* Current
* Easy to start
* Hard to abandon
The first few seconds are critical.
The user should quickly feel:
“This app knows football, and it understands what kind of fan I am.”
15. Product Priorities
The most important priorities are:
1. Great first-time user experience
2. Fast engagement without login friction
3. Intelligent personalization
4. High-quality quiz questions
5. Current football data
6. Excellent UX
7. Very high code quality
8. Strong local development pipeline
9. Efficient battery and hardware usage
10. Maintainable cross-platform architecture
16. Initial Product Hypothesis
The app should not start as a generic quiz app.
It should start as a smart, personalized, current football quiz app.
The main hook could be:
“Test how well you followed football this week.”
This gives the app a reason to be opened repeatedly.
A user may come back daily or weekly to check whether they still know what is happening in football.
